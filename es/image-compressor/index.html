❓ Frequently Asked Questions
Are images sent to any server?

No. All compression happens directly inside the browser. Your files never leave your device and are never uploaded to any server.

What formats are supported?

The compressor supports JPG/JPEG, PNG and WebP. All output is automatically converted to JPEG.

How much compression can be achieved?

Compression typically ranges between 40% and 80% depending on the image and selected quality. High-quality original JPG images respond best to compression.

Does compression affect image quality?

The compressor uses a lossy compression algorithm that reduces file size while preserving acceptable visual quality. The more compression applied, the slightly lower the quality.

Does the compressor preserve PNG transparency?

No. This compressor converts PNG to JPEG, and JPEG does not support transparency. If your image has a transparent background it will automatically be replaced with a white background. To preserve PNG transparency, use a dedicated PNG compressor such as TinyPNG.

What is the best quality level for compression?

80%–85% is ideal for sharing online and via email — excellent balance between quality and size. 60%–75% for greater size reduction with acceptable quality. 90%+ for print use. The default value of 80% is suitable for most cases.

Why might the compressed image be larger than the original?

This happens when the original image is already well compressed or very small. Also, large PNGs converted to JPEG at high quality may have similar sizes. Solution: gradually lower the quality level until you achieve the desired saving.

Image Compressor — Reduce Size Without Losing Quality

This tool compresses images directly in your browser using the HTML5 Canvas API. No files are ever uploaded to any server — your photos stay completely private. Supports JPEG, PNG and WebP. Drag and drop your image, choose a quality level, and download the result instantly.

Choosing the right quality level

Quality levels 70-85% give the best balance — typically 60-80% smaller file size with barely noticeable quality reduction. Below 60% you may see visible artifacts. Above 90% the file size reduction becomes minimal. For profile photos and blog images, 75-80% is the recommended starting point.

Why image size affects SEO and performance

A 4 MB photo forces every visitor to download 4 MB just to view your page. Compressed to 400 KB at similar visual quality, the page loads 10× faster. Google's Core Web Vitals (LCP) directly measures how fast your largest image loads — smaller images improve both user experience and search ranking.

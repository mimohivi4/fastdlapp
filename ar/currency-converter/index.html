📌 How does the currency converter work?

This converter fetches live exchange rates from ExchangeRate-API (open.er-api.com), updated every 4 hours. The last loaded rates are saved in your browser automatically for offline use.

The US Dollar (USD) is used as the base currency, and all other conversion rates are calculated from it.

The rates shown are mid-market reference exchange rates — with no bank markup or hidden fees.

❓ FAQ — Currency Converter
What is today's USD to OMR exchange rate?

The Omani Rial is pegged to the US Dollar at a fixed official rate: 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD (or 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR). This rate has been fixed since 1986 by the Central Bank of Oman and does not change.

Is the Omani Rial pegged to the US Dollar?

Yes. The Omani Rial (OMR) has been pegged to the US Dollar (USD) at a fixed rate since 1986: 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. This peg provides exceptional stability and makes OMR one of the strongest currencies in the Gulf region.

What is the difference between mid-market rate and bank rate?

The mid-market rate is the reference rate for a currency in financial markets — with no profit margin. Banks and exchange offices add a spread on top of this rate, which is why their rate differs from what this converter shows.

What is the SAR and AED rate against OMR?

Approximately: 1 OMR ≈ 9.75 SAR (Saudi Riyal) and 1 OMR ≈ 9.55 AED (UAE Dirham). Both SAR and AED are pegged to the US Dollar at near-fixed rates, so their conversions with OMR are relatively stable.

Does the converter work offline?

Yes, partially. When the connection is lost, the converter uses the last rates automatically saved in your browser (from the last successful update). If no saved rates exist, approximate fallback rates are used for reference only. The status bar below the result shows which data type is being displayed.

Currency Converter — How Exchange Rates Work

This converter uses live exchange rates updated every 4 hours via a public rates API. Rates shown are mid-market rates — the midpoint between buying and selling rates. Banks and exchange offices typically add a 1-3% spread, so the rate you receive in practice will differ slightly from what is shown here.

Gulf currencies pegged to the US Dollar

  • AED (UAE Dirham) — fixed at 3.6725 per USD since 1997
  • SAR (Saudi Riyal) — fixed at 3.75 per USD since 1986
  • OMR (Omani Rial) — fixed at 0.3845 per USD since 1986
  • JOD (Jordanian Dinar) — fixed at 0.709 per USD since 1995

Mid-market rate vs. bank rate

The mid-market rate is the "real" exchange rate. Banks add a margin of 1-5% on top. For large transfers, compare rates from multiple providers. Online money transfer services (Wise, Revolut) typically offer rates closer to the mid-market rate than traditional banks.

❓ US Tipping FAQs
How much should you tip at a restaurant in the US?

18–20% is the standard for sit-down restaurants. 15% signals poor service; 25%+ signals exceptional appreciation. Many Americans simply double the tax line as an easy estimate.

Do you tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Technically the tip should be on the pre-tax subtotal, but many diners tip on the full amount including tax. Either is acceptable — servers appreciate generosity.

What is automatic gratuity for large groups?

Most US restaurants add an automatic 18% gratuity for parties of 6 or more. Always check the bill before adding a tip — you may be double-tipping.

Why is tipping so important in the US?

US federal law allows restaurants to pay tipped workers a base wage as low as $2.13/hour — servers depend on tips for their income. In most states, tips make up the majority of a server's pay.

Is it OK to not tip in the US?

Not tipping at a sit-down restaurant is considered rude and harms the server's livelihood. If service was poor, a 10–12% tip sends the message without leaving zero. No tip is appropriate only for truly unacceptable service.

Tip Calculator for the US — How Tipping Works in America

In the United States, tipping is not optional at sit-down restaurants — it is a social and economic expectation. Servers earn a federal tipped minimum wage of $2.13/hour, and tips make up the majority of their income. A standard tip of 18–20% on the pre-tax subtotal is the baseline for good service.

How to use this calculator
